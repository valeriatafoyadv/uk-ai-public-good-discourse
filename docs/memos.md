# Analytical memos: a sample

Round 1.1 was manual: for each document in the corpus's first intake, the author read it and
wrote a memo against the seven core questions (BENEFICIARY, MECHANISM, SAFEGUARD,
RESPONSIBILITY, PROJECTED_FUTURE, ACTANTS, NATURALISED_ORDER) before any of it was
pipeline-assisted. That NVivo project and its full memos are not in this repository. The six below
are drawn from it and lightly edited for readability — condensed into prose, not into new
findings — so the manual round can be seen, not only the pipeline that came after it.

The memos cover the 35 documents (36 counting a repeat pass) admitted in the corpus's first
intake, written between 24 August and 5 September 2026; the 31 documents added afterward were
coded directly by the pipeline, without an individual memo. Coverage across the 36 is uneven — a
short document might get a full close read, a long one a scan of its structure and a keyword
search, noted here each time as "what I did" — and that unevenness is reported as a limitation
in the dissertation itself, not smoothed over here.

## D1 · AI Playbook for the UK Government

*10 February 2025 · [`2025-02-10_STRAT_GDS_AIPlaybookUKGovernment`](corpus.md)*

The first document coded, and only its ethics section. The Playbook glosses "societal wellbeing
and public good" as one of six ethical principles, and the memo pulled out its three clearest
lines: AI should "deliver tangible benefits to members of the public and the economy"; an AI
solution must "benefit the public" to count as good value for money; and "the potential public
benefits that we can achieve using AI are wide-ranging." Against the four structural questions,
the memo reads the beneficiary as "members of the public and the economy," the mechanism as a
list — productivity, access to services, health interventions, personalised training,
sustainability, reduced inequality — and the safeguards as harm minimisation and a stated
willingness to terminate a project if the risk is too high. Responsibility falls to civil servants,
written in the second person, with a note that each department is separately responsible for its
own AI strategy. The three narrative questions (projected future, hero or threat, naturalised
order) were left for a second pass.

## D3 · Blueprint for Modern Digital Government

*21 January 2025 · [`2025-01-21_STRAT_GDS_BlueprintModernDigitalGov`](corpus.md)*

The Blueprint is where "harness the power of AI for public good" first appears as a section
heading, and the memo traces the mechanism straight from the text: "AI has enormous potential to
improve lives, drive growth and increase productivity... we must harness this by integrating AI
and automation safely and securely within the design and delivery of public services." The memo
also caught the document's own intertextual claim — a year later, the Roadmap for Modern Digital
Government describes itself as detailing "the foundational steps we have taken since launching the
blueprint... one year ago" — which is one of the links carried into the citation network. Reading
was a first pass over the section structure, flagged for a full read later; the six-point plan for
digital reform was located and logged for Round 2.

## D8 · AI Opportunities Action Plan

*13 January 2025 · [`2025-01-13_STRAT_DSIT_AIOpportunitiesActionPlan`](corpus.md)*

The most-cited document in the network (in-degree 25) has the thinnest Round 1.1 memo, and the
memo says so plainly: "I quickly scanned and searched for the term but did not find anything. I
will have to read in detail. Did not code much." What it does record is the Plan's own framing —
"a bold roadmap for maximising AI's potential to drive growth and deliver real benefits to people
across the UK" — and a hypothesis, marked as a hypothesis, that Recommendation 33's call for
"two-way partnerships with AI vendors and startups" is what the later memoranda of understanding
put into practice. The dissertation reports that hypothesis as a hypothesis, and reports the
memo's own coverage gap in its limitations.

## D16 · Same Name, New Ambitions

*27 January 2025 · [`2025-01-27_BLOG_GDS_SameNameNewAmbitions`](corpus.md)*

The blog post announcing GDS's own formation, read and coded in full. The memo notes it was
published six days after the Blueprint, and that the post reaches for the Blueprint's own language
— "a responsible, ambitious stance on using AI" — to describe the new institution's mandate. It
also records why the State of Digital Government Review sits in the corpus at all: the post states
that the Blueprint "was informed by" that review, which is the intertextual link that justified
including it. The term itself does not appear in this document.

## D35 · Tackling AI Security Risks

*14 February 2025 · [`2025-02-14_PRGOV_DSIT_TacklingAISecurityRisks`](corpus.md)*

Read in full despite zero occurrences of the phrase, because it announces the Anthropic memorandum
of understanding and so completes a family comparison the memo was tracking: OpenAI and Google
DeepMind each have a government-side release alongside their memorandum, and this is Anthropic's.
The memo's finding is that the announcement frames the partnership through national security
rather than public service — the AI Safety Institute is renamed the AI Security Institute in the
same document — and that "citizens" appears seven times, five of them casting citizens as objects
of protection from harm ("protecting citizens from crime," "ensuring its citizens are safe and
protected") rather than recipients of a service. Same noun, a different role. The Anthropic CEO's
own contribution uses "UK residents," a beneficiary term outside the eight the memo was tracking
for elsewhere in the corpus.

## D36 · Data and AI Ethics Framework

*18 December 2025 · [`2025-12-18_STRAT_GDS_DataAIEthicsFramework`](corpus.md)*

Read in full, with a second pass (Dx in the original memo numbering) the next day. The document
was retitled from the Data Ethics Framework and given four new principles, one of which, societal
impact, is glossed as being "about how the development and use of data and data-driven
technologies impact wider society. For example, by helping to solve societal challenges and
deliver public good." The memo flags this as a finding in its own right: it is the clearest
instance anywhere in the corpus of "public good" being defined by proximity to a named ethical
principle rather than to the umbrella phrase's own two surface forms, and it is read in the
dissertation as one of the sites the phrase's meaning gets built from.
